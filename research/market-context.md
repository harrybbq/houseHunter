## 1. Closing-price premium over "offers over"

### What the data measures
Scottish sale data is almost always reported as **% of Home Report (HR) valuation**, not % of the "offers over" asking price. Agents commonly set the asking price at or below the HR value; this is market convention, not a sourced statistic. When asking < HR, price ÷ asking is higher than price ÷ HR. I found no published series of price ÷ asking for Glasgow, and no sourced figure for the typical gap between asking and HR.

## 3. LBTT for first-time buyers (Revenue Scotland / Scottish Budget)

**Your model is correct:** nil to £175k, 2% to £250k, 5% to £325k.

- Standard residential bands (effective from 1 Apr 2026, unchanged for 2026-27): 0% to £145,000; 2% £145,001–£250,000; 5% £250,001–£325,000; 10% £325,001–£750,000; 12% above £750,000. Source: [Scottish Budget 2026-27, Tax Policy chapter (gov.scot)](https://www.gov.scot/publications/scottish-budget-2026-2027/pages/4/).
- **First-time buyer relief** raises the nil-rate band from £145,000 to **£175,000**. The saving is up to £600. There is **no price cap**, and the relief also applies above £175k. Qualification: never owned a dwelling anywhere, and the property will be the buyer's only or main residence. Sources: [Revenue Scotland LBTT3048 First-time buyer relief](https://revenue.scot/taxes/land-buildings-transaction-tax/lbtt-legislation-guidance/lbtt3001-exemptions-reliefs/lbtt3010-tax-reliefs/lbtt3048-first-time-buyer-relief), [Revenue Scotland Residential property](https://revenue.scot/taxes/land-buildings-transaction-tax/residential-property) (accessed 29 Sep 2026).
- **ADS** is 8%. It does not apply to a first-time buyer. Same Budget source.
- **Announced changes:** none for 2026-27. **Risk for Apr–Jul 2027 completions:** 2027-28 rates will be set in the Scottish Budget, normally published Dec/Jan. The Programme for Government 2026-31 (1 Sep 2026) lists no LBTT change ([gov.scot news, 1 Sep 2026](https://www.gov.scot/news/ambitious-for-scotland-2/)). Re-check in January 2027.

## 4. Home Report valuations, the "valuation gap", and high-LTV mortgages

### How often the price exceeds the HR value
In Glasgow, "down-valuation" usually means the agreed price is above the HR valuation. It rarely means a surveyor cutting a value after the offer. There is no published statistic on how often lenders or surveyors value below the agreed price. The best proxy is ESPC's average of **106.6–108.3% of HR** across Glasgow East, South and West in 2026 (§1). That average implies **most tenement sales in these areas complete above the HR figure**, typically by 5–10%, with a tail of 15–25%.

### How lenders and high-LTV buyers handle it (mechanism, not advice)
- Lenders lend on the **lower of price and valuation**. Scottish lenders usually rely on the HR valuation, though some instruct their own valuation. Any amount paid above the valuation must come from the buyer's own cash, **on top of** the deposit. Sources: [Gillespie Macandrew, 2026](https://gillespiemacandrew.co.uk/news-insights/2026/home-reports-in-scotland-explained-a-complete-guide-for-home-buyers/), [Zoopla guide](https://www.zoopla.co.uk/discover/selling/what-to-do-if-a-home-valuation-is-lower-than-the-offer/) (secondary).
- Worked example (my arithmetic). HR value £200k, price £215k, 90% LTV: the mortgage is 90% × £200k = £180k, so the cash needed is £35k (a 10% deposit on the valuation plus the £15k gap), not £21.5k. At 95% LTV the cash needed is £25k.
- Common approaches reported by buyers and advisers: cap bids at an amount that keeps the gap within available cash; ask the broker for a lender whose own valuer may "retype" or revalue; move to a lower-LTV tier; or target properties where the asking price sits below the HR value. Sources: [MoneySavingExpert forum thread](https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/5805748/paying-over-home-report-value-scotland), [McKendry Dunion](https://mckendrydunion.co.uk/first-time-buyers/offers-over-home-report/) (secondary/anecdotal).
- Home Reports must be under 12 weeks old when a property is first marketed. Lenders may require a re-inspection or re-dated valuation if the report is stale. Source: [mygov.scot Home Report guidance](https://www.mygov.scot/home-report) (general guidance; not fetched in this session).

### 95% LTV availability, 2026
- The UK Mortgage Guarantee Scheme was made **permanent in July 2025** ("Freedom to Buy"). It covers 91–95% LTV loans on homes up to £600k. Source: [Financial Reporter](https://www.financialreporter.co.uk/government-confirms-launch-of-permanent-freedom-to-buy-mortgage-scheme.html).

Source: [HomeOwners Alliance/MAB, 29 Sep 2026](https://hoa.org.uk/first-time-buyer-mortgage-rates/). Product counts in Dec 2025: 917 products at 90% LTV and 476 at 95% LTV. Source: [Moneyfacts, 8 Dec 2025](https://www.moneyfactsgroup.co.uk/media-centre/group/lenders-slash-rates-and-improve-choice-for-borrowers/).

## 5. Seasonality

- Listings **peak in spring (March–May)** and stay high through summer into early autumn. Source: [Rettie Glasgow West End report, 31 May 2023](https://www.rettie.co.uk/property-research-services/glasgow-west-end-housing-market) (secondary, dated). RoS transaction volumes usually spike in April–May, but these are completion dates and lag the offer by about 2–3 months.
- **December** has the fewest new listings and buyers. In the two weeks after Christmas, buyer enquiries rise about 57% and new listings about 81%. Source: [Rightmove HPI, 19 Jan 2026](https://www.rightmove.co.uk/news/content/uploads/2026/01/Rightmove-HPI-19-January-Final.pdf) (GB-wide; figures via search extract, PDF not fetched).
- A claim that **February** is the best month for sellers (68.9% of homes finding a buyer) appeared in a search extract with unclear attribution, so treat it as unverified. It is consistent with strong early-year demand.
- Southside agents described September 2025 as closing-date heavy ([SPC Sep 2025](https://scottishpropertycentre.net/blog/market-update-from-our-glasgow-and-argyll-branches-september-2025/48048)).

**For the Feb–Jul 2027 window:** supply peaks in April–June. Competition is high from February to May, after the post-Christmas surge. **July** (summer holidays) is likely the least contested month in the window. June and July keep reasonable supply while competition eases. Mid-November to mid-December has the least competition overall but falls outside the window. No Glasgow-specific monthly premium series was found.

## 6. Tenement-specific risks

### Legal framework (primary)
- **Tenement Management Scheme (TMS)**, Tenements (Scotland) Act 2004 Sch. 1. It applies wherever the title deeds are silent. Scheme property includes the roof, external walls, foundations and load-bearing structure. Decisions are taken by **majority, one vote per flat**. Maintenance costs are shared **equally**, unless the largest flat is more than 1.5 times the floor area of the smallest, in which case costs are shared by floor area. Any owner may instruct emergency work. Owners may appoint a manager (factor). Source: [legislation.gov.uk, Sch. 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2004/11/schedule/1).
  - **Check:** do the title deeds override the TMS? Deeds may impose different shares, for example by rateable value or feu-charter proportions.
- **Liability on purchase (s.12):** the buyer is jointly liable with the seller for costs of already-decided or completed work **only if a Notice of Potential Liability for Costs (NPLC)** was registered at least 14 days before the buyer takes entry. An NPLC lasts 3 years and can be renewed. Sources: [s.12](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2004/11/section/12), [Under One Roof](https://underoneroof.scot/notice-of-potential-liability-for-costs/).
  - **Check:** the solicitor's title and register search for NPLCs; the factor's letter confirming arrears, approved works and any float or sinking fund; and the minutes of recent owners' meetings.

### Condition data (primary)
- Scottish House Condition Survey 2024 (published 24 Feb 2026). **68% of pre-1919 dwellings** have disrepair to critical elements, 32% have urgent disrepair, and 7% have urgent and extensive disrepair (up from 4%). Across all dwellings the figures are 48% and 19%. Source: [gov.scot SHCS 2024, ch.5](https://www.gov.scot/publications/scottish-house-condition-survey-2024-key-findings/pages/5-housing-conditions/).
- About 900,000 tenement flats in Scotland, about 45% with critical-element disrepair. Source: [Under One Roof, 17 Dec 2025](https://underoneroof.scot/a-workable-model-for-building-reserve-funds-new-report-out-now/).

### Factor fees (secondary, indicative)
- Glasgow traditional tenements typically pay **£600–£1,500 per flat per year** including buildings insurance. Major works are billed separately. The management fee alone is about £150–£400. Sources: [MoneyScot, 2026/27](https://moneyscot.co.uk/blog/factoring-fees-scottish-tenements), [Compare Factors](https://comparefactors.co.uk/guides/self-factoring-scotland/).
  - **Check:** whether the factor is on the Property Factor Register; the written statement of services; whether insurance is common or individual; the float amount (often £100–£300, refundable on sale); and whether a sinking fund exists.

### Roof and stonework costs (secondary, low confidence)
- Sandstone repairs cost about £300–£900 for a localised repair and £800–£2,500 for part-elevation repointing. Full façade restoration starts around £3,000 and can go well beyond £10,000 before scaffolding. Tenement scaffolding adds materially to this. Source: [A&M Sandstone, 26 May 2026](https://aandmsandstonerestoration.co.uk/how-much-does-sandstone-restoration-cost-in-glasgow/).
- Typical life cycles: slate roofs and cast-iron rainwater goods 50–100 years; chimney heads and lead work 40–50 years. Source: [Glasgow Roofing Service](https://www.glasgowroofingservice.com/tenement-roof-repairs/) (undated).
- I found **no reliable published per-flat figure for a full tenement re-roof**. Planning assumption only, not sourced: a full re-slate plus chimneys with scaffolding on an 8-flat close commonly runs into five figures per flat. Get quotes and factor history rather than relying on this.
- Glasgow City Council has limited grant help for common repairs, and snippets indicate works under £5,000 per owner are ineligible. The page returned 403 and this is unverified: [GCC Grants for Property Repairs](https://www.glasgow.gov.uk/grantsforpropertyrepairs).

### Reform proposals and 2026 legislation status
- The Parliamentary Working Group on Tenement Maintenance (final report June 2019) recommended **five-yearly building condition inspections**, **compulsory owners' associations** and **building reserve funds**. Source: [BEFS](https://www.befs.org.uk/policy-topics/buildings-maintenance-2/).
- The **Scottish Law Commission Report No. 270 on compulsory owners' associations**, with a **draft Tenements (Amendment) (Scotland) Bill**, was published around Dec 2025/Jan 2026. It makes about 95 recommendations. Each association would appoint a manager, hold an AGM, approve a budget and note information on the register. Sources: [Citylets blog, 3 Feb 2026](https://www.citylets.co.uk/blog/whats-new-for-tenement-maintenance-in-2026/), [SLC BRIA PDF, Feb 2026](https://www.scotlawcom.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-02/BRIA_-_Tenements_Amendment_Scotland_Bill.pdf).
- Under One Roof (17 Dec 2025) proposed a model for mandatory reserve funds.
- SNP, Labour, Lib Dem and Green 2026 manifestos all backed tenement reform. Source: [Scottish Housing News, 5 May 2026](https://www.scottishhousingnews.com/articles/four-major-parties-make-manifesto-commitments-on-tenement-reform).
- **Status as of Sep 2026: not legislated.** The Programme for Government 2026-31 Year 1 legislative programme (1 Sep 2026) lists nine bills and **no tenements bill**. It does include a Buildings (Heating and Energy Performance) and Heat Networks Bill and a Council Tax (Scotland) Bill. Source: [gov.scot, 1 Sep 2026](https://www.gov.scot/news/ambitious-for-scotland-2/). No compulsory five-yearly survey or reserve fund is likely to be in force by 2027. Future owners may still face them, which would mean reserve contributions of an unknown amount.

### Buyer checklist
1. Read the Home Report Single Survey for category 3 or 2 items on the roof, chimneys, stonework, rainwater goods and damp or timber. Check the EPC; heating and energy performance rules are coming via the new Buildings Bill.
2. Get the factor's name, annual fees, float, arrears, and any approved or pending common works. Ask for owners' meeting minutes.
3. Solicitor search for an NPLC. Confirm whether the TMS or the deeds apply, and what share of costs the flat carries.
4. Check whether common buildings insurance is in place and adequate.
5. For top-floor flats, look closely at roof condition. For ground and basement flats, look at damp and drainage.
6. Look at the close and back court for condition and for signs of absentee or landlord owners, which make it harder to reach majority decisions and collect shares.

## Implications for the buyer's model (suggested defaults, not advice)

**Closing uplift over the Home Report value** (central, with a low–high band for sensitivity). All the Glasgow data measures price ÷ HR value, not price ÷ asking. The buyer sees the Home Report before offering, so the model should key the uplift off the HR value. Treat asking → HR as a separate documented assumption: default HR ≈ asking × 1.00, with ×1.05 as an optimistic case (see the risk band below). I found no sourced figure for the typical gap between asking and HR in Glasgow. One unsourced claim puts asking 10–15% below HR, and if that were true the uplift over asking would be higher than these figures.

| Area | Central (× HR) | Band | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Southside G41/G42 | **×1.09** | ×1.03–1.20 | ESPC Glasgow South 108.3% of HR (Mar–Aug 2026); agents report 12% average and 17–21% at closing dates |
| Dennistoun G31 | **×1.07** | ×1.00–1.15 | ESPC Glasgow East 106.6% of HR (12 months to Aug 2026) |
| West End G11/G12 | **×1.07** | ×1.00–1.15 | ESPC Glasgow West 106.7%; Hyndland −5% YoY suggests softer at the top end |

If the model applies the uplift to the asking price and asking ≈ HR, then ×1.00–1.10 is reasonable for G31 and G11/G12. For G41/G42 the top of 1.10 is roughly the average, not the ceiling.

**Down-valuation / valuation-gap risk band.** Assume HR value ≈ asking × 1.00 as the default, with ×1.05 as an optimistic case. The mortgage is then LTV × min(price, HR value), and the cash needed is price − mortgage.

| Band | Price relative to HR value | Cash gap on top of the deposit |
|---|---|---|
| Green | ≤ HR | none |
| Amber | HR to HR +5% | up to ~5% of price extra |
| Red | > HR +5% | gap > 5% of price. Typical of a contested Southside closing date. |
